## Changes
- **The rule.** The `verification-contract` rule opened with a sentence that held the coverage rule's question. It now opens by judging each criterion's own check, and it sends the other question to coverage.
- **The table of reviewers.** The attack skill's table gains a column naming what each reviewer reads, which decides who owns a rule.
<details><summary>Design notes</summary>

The `verification-contract` rule opened by saying that every material commitment has a feasible check. That sentence holds the coverage rule's question, so the reviewer that owns `verification-contract` filed coverage gaps under it. On the pilot's second case, 18 of 20 runs raised such objections under the rule as it was.

Each reviewer in the attack panel is defined by the unit it reads. One reads a single criterion on its own shape. One reads the set against the document's promises. One reads the set against itself. One asks a single criterion what it leaves untested. That unit decides which reviewer owns a rule. Two reviewers may share ground when one asks about a criterion and the other about the set.

The measurement used the pilot's second case, which is a realistic document of eight criteria with one planted defect, and a corrected control. Ten fresh runs per document read prompts emitted from each version of the standard. 10 of 10 defective runs objected to the planted criterion under the planted rule, before the change and after it. 1 of 10 defective runs objected elsewhere, down from 10 of 10. 9 of 10 control runs returned an empty report, up from 2 of 10. The one control objection left cites a contradiction the test document carries in both versions.

No reviewer prompt changed. No existing attack record is reopened, because the record check binds to the document's revision and never to the rule text. Other sentences in the attack skill were shortened to keep its body under the size cap.

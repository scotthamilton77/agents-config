# Review-panel iteration strategy

**Date:** 2026-08-20, amended 2026-08-22 · **Status:** settled design; PANEL-A1–A4 and B1–B11 implemented (PR #579), the circuits amendment (PANEL-B12–B15) not yet
**Drives:** `agents-config-9k9.17.9` (cost-driver reassessment, PANEL-A1–A4), `agents-config-9k9.17.16` (iteration-strategy reassessment, PANEL-B1–B11), `agents-config-9k9.17.18` (sequenced circuits and the spec-code class, PANEL-B12–B15), with upstream companion `agents-config-9k9.229`.
**Evidence base:** PRs #402/#403 (first two campaigns) and #556/#557 (9k9.215 campaigns, per-lens convergence data). Settled in a grilling session; the tracker items carry the observation detail.

## 1. Goals

1. **Efficient and comprehensive** detection of quality issues across work shapes, including mixed targets (e.g. a skill combining prose and scripts).
2. **No compensation for missing mechanical verification.** The panel assumes acceptance criteria (ACs) were turned into mechanical tests upstream and that those tests passed. It checks for *evidence* of that and bounces targets that lack it; it never re-derives it.
3. **Minimum force, including zero.** Force is minimized against expected total cost — tokens spent plus escaped-defect interventions — not tokens alone. A zero-force verdict is legitimate only when the staffing decision can justify that no lens applies — the recorded justification (§4) is the operational test — and it is always recorded, never silent.

What is not broken survives: adversarial multi-model review stays; the settled-items ledger stays (no rebutted finding was ever re-filed) and extends to rebutted design-decision attacks.

## 2. Root causes of the four observed cost drivers (PANEL-A1)

| Prose artifacts accrete under fixing; same defect class re-minted on newly written text (#402: 388→500 lines) | The fix side is entirely ungoverned — the contract instructs reviewers only, so fixes layer qualifiers onto wrong text instead of replacing it | Panel scope — the contract extends to a fix-dispatch contract (§6) |

## 3. Precondition gate

Emission refuses a target that lacks recorded evidence that its profile's declared mechanical gates ran green at the head SHA under review, naming the missing or failing gate. Evidence is an execution record — gate name, exit status, head SHA — never a bare assertion that the gate passed; failed, malformed, or unverifiable evidence is refused the same way as absent evidence. Evidence bound to any other head is refused as stale. The refusal verb is **bounce upstream** — the panel does not review around the gap, and it does not judge whether the upstream gates were the right ones.

The mechanical gates are per-profile, not one rule: an AC-derived test gate for typed code; doc-lint, the cosmetic pre-pass, and a criteria artifact whose acceptance criteria survived an ac-attack round for specs; doc-lint plus the cosmetic pre-pass for general docs; nothing at all for a no-gate profile, which passes on empty evidence.

Producing this evidence is upstream's obligation, encoded as de facto ACs on the planning and implementation phases (`agents-config-9k9.229`): criteria red-test-convertible and ac-attack-survived before implementation; profile named at planning time; gate evidence recorded at the delivered head; scope boundaries stated in the claim. Deploy order matters: the gate is not enforced on real campaigns before that encoding lands, or every legitimate target bounces.

## 4. Classes, profiles, and staffing

**Classes stay coarse** — typed-code, spec-code, spec, prose — because a class is heavy: its own lens roster, its own round per mixed target, its own verdict. A mixed target partitions by class into one staffed round and one verdict per present class, each finding attributed to exactly one class partition; a class partition whose staffing decision is a justified zero contributes its terminal staffing record in place of a verdict. Tier vocabulary is the deployed contract's: a lens declares `frontier` (hard-reasoning seats) or `mid` (mechanical-walk seats), and "frontier-tier seats" throughout this record means the lenses a class declares `frontier`. Every roster lens carries the declaration; a roster holding an undeclared-tier lens is refused before any staffing, force-ceiling, or sweep determination reads it (PANEL-B15). Sub-type sensitivity lives in a **profile table** the staffing step reads: one row per artifact type carrying default staffing (the lens subset staffing starts from), force ceiling (the maximum review force the profile permits — staffing may subtract below it but never exceed it; a zero ceiling is the zero-force default), a precondition set, and an explicit per-row no-gate marker where preconditions are waived. Minimum rows: prototype (the one no-gate minimum row — zero-force default, no preconditions), changelog (mechanical-only), agent-instruction prose (skills, rules, CLAUDE.md — mandates derived from the writing-skills discipline rather than freshly minted lens text; preconditions doc-lint plus the cosmetic pre-pass), spec (preconditions doc-lint, the cosmetic pre-pass, and the attacked criteria artifact of §3), general docs (preconditions doc-lint plus the cosmetic pre-pass), typed-code (precondition the AC-derived test gate of §3). The cosmetic pre-pass is PANEL-A2's mechanical home: a spell/grammar/link checker declared as a precondition gate and run upstream like any other, so typo-class defects are caught before the panel and consume no lens seat and no round. The table is validated before use: a missing minimum row, a duplicate type, an incomplete row, or a typed-code row declaring zero preconditions is refused — the no-gate marker is the only route to an empty precondition set, so a profile cannot quietly authorize reviewing around missing tests. An unlisted type picks a listed profile explicitly and records the choice and the reason — never an improvised lens set; a target whose profile cannot be resolved at all is refused as a resolution error, distinct from a no-gate pass. A new sub-type is a row edit, not a design event. Splitting the panel into per-shape skills instead moves classification upstream to an invoker with less information, multiplies deployed surface, and duplicates the round/verdict machinery that must stay identical — the mixed-target case (one round per class over a partitioned target) is the argument for one skill, and it is already the incumbent architecture.

**Spec-code is the fourth class.** Code authored *as* specification — interface stubs, data shapes, prototypes pinning a design decision: the scaffold artifacts D4 mints, compilable stubs and deliberately failing tests, contract-only — reviews under its own roster, never typed-code's, because a production-code lens on specification code is a findings generator with the wrong contract: "no error handling", "nothing imports this" — true observations, wrong findings, emitted indefinitely. The roster attacks interface quality (each signature and data shape judged as the commitment it pins), the contract-only boundary (an implementation body promotes a consequence into a decision before the evidence for it exists — a finding here, where on typed-code a body is just code), and pinning adequacy (the deliberately failing tests pin the decisions the criteria enumerate). Classification carries a **declaration burden**, not a mechanical test: code is typed-code until the round's records state why the artifact cannot ship — not deployed, not imported by production paths, not gated as product. The presumption faces spec-code because its rules are softer, and a soft class adoptable by labeling is a shelter for weak production code; the recorded cannot-ship rationale is audit-attackable, the same seam as the sweep's target-shaped rationale. A mechanical reachability or CI-membership test was considered and rejected: this repository's own CI gates non-production code (the content-tests suites), and the deployed skill runs in projects whose gates it cannot cite. Termination is weaker by construction — spec-code has no mutation evidence, its tests fail on purpose — so its precondition gate is parse-level (the artifact compiles or parses, recorded like any gate execution) and `fixed` keeps evidence optional, as schema v3 already provides for every class without a test gate. Its profile row ships in the table without joining the minimum-row floor: the floor guards rows whose absence breaks resolution of common targets, and a design-phase target names its type explicitly.

**Staffing is a first-class, per-round recorded artifact**: a foreign model — one from outside the reviewing session's own vendor family, for independence — at the routing table's mid tier recommends which of the class's lenses actually apply to this target. The record names the recommending model, and carries a rationale per *excluded* roster lens — a blanket "not needed" cannot silently drop the security seat from a target full of authentication logic. Interactive sessions present the recommendation to the user, and the user's final edit is what gets recorded as the decision; non-interactive runs record the recommendation and proceed. Staffing is **subtract-only** — the class roster is the ceiling. The verdict references the staffing record, and validation is two-layer: verdict lens coverage is checked against the staffing record, and the staffing record is checked against the class roster — every roster lens is either staffed or carries its exclusion rationale — so a silently dropped seat fails at the staffing layer rather than escaping both checks. A zero-lens decision with non-blank justification is itself the terminal record and no verdict exists for it; a zero-lens outcome arising from a missing or failed recommendation is refused instead — a staffing failure is never mistaken for a deliberate, justified zero-lens decision.

**Embedded prose** (comments, docstrings — the most persistent defect class on #557, reviewed like code while rotting like prose) is a lens duty, not a fourth class: the typed-code documentation-quality mandate explicitly owns narration, drift, and history-vs-decision rot, with a stopping condition under which each finding names the misreading the comment causes. A merely brief comment is not a finding. The fix-side narration clause (§6) attacks the same class from the other flank. That clause is also the general form PANEL-A4 demands: a stopping condition is a mandate clause naming the observable each finding must cite — the misreading caused, for embedded prose; the failure the missing test would have caught, for test-adequacy — and a mandate lacking such a clause fails content review at implementation.

## 5. Round lifecycle

1. **Round 1 — whole artifact**, staffed per the staffing decision. A clean, complete full round 1 whose staffing retained every frontier-tier seat is terminal directly: it is already a whole-artifact clean read by the sweep's own seats. If round-1 staffing subtracted any frontier-tier seat, the terminal sweep below still applies.
2. **Rounds ≥2 — delta-scoped by default.** Each surviving lens reads only the change since the head it last judged, plus the disposition ledger. Staffing is re-evaluated every round from the finding trend and the nature of the fixes; lenses that went clean drop, and a lens whose delta is empty — nothing changed since the head it judged — is not staffed at all, which is cheaper than an empty dispatch. A newly staffed lens (no prior head) reads full. The **accretion trigger** forces a full rescope: fixes since the last full read have grown beyond trivial (the same implementation-defined, edge-tested boundary as §6 clause 3), or a finding shows an original assumption was wrong.
3. **Terminal sweep — exactly one whole-artifact pass** before terminal-clean is declared on any campaign that reached its zero-blocking round through deltas. The exit door has one full re-read built into the frame, because the late finds on both evidence campaigns (#403 round 3, #557 round 6 on code unchanged since round 1) are invisible to delta scoping by construction. The sweep flies under its own **pre-sweep staffing decision**, made with the same machinery as any round's (§4) but subtract-only from the class's frontier-tier seats — the profile's force ceiling does not bound this roster, because the sweep is its own recorded force decision, which is also what lets a mechanical-only profile exist without being condemned to frontier spend. The default is the full sweep and the burden faces subtraction: a trend-shaped rationale ("the campaign looks clean", "every lens went green") is inadmissible, because clean-looking delta rounds are precisely the blindness the sweep compensates; a valid rationale is target-shaped — it names what in this campaign's changes gives the seat nothing to judge. A zero-seat sweep decision with non-blank justification is itself the terminal record, exactly as §4's zero-force decision is; a mechanical-only profile such as changelog typically ends this way. The sweep is framed blocking-only ("confirm no blocking defect exists" — a verdict, not a findings hunt) and runs with the ledger loaded. A sweep whose findings are all advisory has confirmed exactly what it was asked: the advisories route to the backlog as advisories always do, and the terminal declaration proceeds — terminal-clean reads zero mechanical findings on a complete, unhalted round, not a literally empty findings array. A class whose roster declares no frontier-tier seat at all escalates to the human rather than declaring terminal-clean — a structural absence is not a judged zero. Sweep findings re-enter the normal fix → delta-verify loop; no second sweep follows unless the accretion trigger re-arms it.

Making per-round full re-reads the default instead is what produced the churn: constant re-litigation surface, and exhausted seats turning into findings-generators (#557's security seat sat silent four rounds, then burst with rebutted design attacks).

### Sequenced circuits over mixed targets

A mixed target's class partitions (§4, PANEL-B10) do not run concurrently. The staffing machinery also emits a **circuit plan** — an ordered grouping of the present partitions into circuits, with a recorded rationale — and the campaign runs each circuit's rounds (this section's lifecycle, per class) to termination before the next circuit's lenses read anything, so a later circuit reviews artifacts describing settled work rather than mid-flight work. One guideline generates the order, and it is a guideline, not a rule: **fix-ripple** — the circuit whose fixes would invalidate the most other circuits' reviews runs first. Typed-code before prose is an instance (code fixes ripple into every description of the code; prose fixes ripple almost nowhere), and spec-code before spec prose is the same instance one level up (interface fixes ripple into the prose deriving from those interfaces). The evidence behind the ordering is #557's embedded-prose class — re-minted by fix passes four times while prose lenses read mid-flight code — and #556's adjacent-misreading loop. Enforcement is the two-layer seam the sweep rationale uses: emission of any round over a mixed target consumes a circuit plan with non-blank rationale (refused when absent), and adherence to the guideline is audit-enforced prose. Plan validation is exact-cover twice over: every present class partition occurs in exactly one circuit — omitted, duplicated, unknown, or overlapping membership is refused — and the partitions themselves exactly cover the declared target scope, every in-scope artifact in exactly one class partition. A later circuit begins only when every earlier circuit reached terminal-clean or a justified zero-lens terminal; any other terminal — a halt, an analyst termination, a human escalation — ends the campaign with the pending circuits unrun, recorded as unrun rather than silently skipped. A single-class target collapses to the trivial one-circuit plan and records nothing extra. Circuit count is an output of the plan, not a constant of the design.

**Re-arm.** A later circuit's fix that changes an earlier, terminated circuit's artifacts re-arms that circuit for a delta round — §5's delta scoping since the head it terminated at, never a full re-run. The mechanical re-arm proxy is fresh gate evidence over gate-relevant files: the earlier circuit's profile gates ran again at a new head because that circuit's files changed; a circuit whose profile declares no gates re-arms on the artifact change itself — the head diff over its files — since no gate evidence exists to carry the signal. The re-arm is an obligation, not an option: campaign termination with a qualifying re-arm unconsumed is refused, and the delta round runs first. Simultaneous re-arms — one fix touching several terminated circuits' files — activate in the plan's original order, each to termination before the next. Circuit identity and partition membership fix at the campaign's first emission: a revised plan cannot reset activation counts by renaming, regrouping, or replacing a circuit, and a re-grouped circuit inherits the highest activation count among its constituent partitions. **Each circuit is capped at two activations — its initial run and one re-arm.** A trigger that would start a third activation instead fires the checkpoint machinery (§7) with continuation inadmissible: the analyst returns terminate-bounce-upstream or terminate-escalate-human, never continue. The cap is deliberately low and explicitly temporary harness-development instrumentation: while the evidence base is thin, its job is to surface what pushes a campaign toward a third activation rather than to absorb it; §9 carries its re-evaluation condition.

**Churn diagnosis.** Every campaign termination that is not terminal-clean carries a churn diagnosis: recommendations upstream (criteria, staffing, class contracts, the spec itself) and/or panel-side (lens selection, circuit plan, sequencing) for reducing the observed churn. Each recommendation cites the campaign evidence it reads — a concrete event or trend in the retained records — and names the seam it changes and the change itself; generic advice ("improve the criteria") is an absent diagnosis. The diagnosis lives on the checkpoint record's terminate verdicts — the trend analyst is already the campaign's judgment seat and already cites the evidence a diagnosis reads — including cap-fired checkpoints; the upstream-defect halt is exempt because its indictment already names the remediation. A terminate verdict without a diagnosis is invalid exactly as an uncited verdict is (§7), resolving as escalate-to-human.

## 6. Fix-dispatch contract

A findings round emits a fix dispatch alongside its verdict; a clean round emits none. The dispatch references each blocking finding it addresses — "clean up the affected section" maps to nothing and validates as incomplete. The review contract and the fix contract are one loop with a shared cost structure — the evidence shows the fixer driving the accretion spiral (three of four narration-class recurrences on #557 were written *during* fix passes), and the one lens whose findings decayed to zero and stayed there was the one whose fixes carried mutation evidence. Four clauses:

1. **Smallest net change** that achieves the fix — no over-explaining, no unnecessary code, no pointless prose.
2. **Mutation evidence for code fixes** — the test fails without the fix. In the ledger, `fixed` on a typed-code mechanical finding requires evidence naming the test and the fails-without/passes-with observation, exactly as `rebutted` already requires evidence; a bare "fixed" claim is as inadmissible as a bare rebuttal.
3. **Replacement-first for prose** — prefer replacing incorrect text over adding qualifying text; a fix that grows the artifact states why replacement couldn't achieve it. Non-trivial net growth in a fix round re-staffs the consistency lens next round, diff-scoped over the new text: growth is paid for with reading, not forbidden. The triviality boundary is defined by the implementation and tested at its edges — zero, negative, and boundary-sized net growth each produce a documented rescoping decision. A hard no-net-growth rule forbids the correct fix for any gap finding and invites clarity-destroying compression; a numeric growth budget has the wrong shape — one legitimate section busts it while five scattered qualifiers sneak under it.
4. **Narration sweep** — no transition commentary minted under review pressure ("as before", "has ever claimed"); prose states the current decision, not the fix's history.

## 7. Dispositions, blame, and termination

**Blame split at round assembly.** The assembler (not a new seat — the split is git-blame-assisted and the assembler already touches every finding) attributes each finding: a PR-induced or missed-in-scope finding stays in the campaign; a pre-existing, non-blocking finding transfers out. The disposition set gains **`transferred`**, valid only with a pre-existing blame attribution whose evidence includes the provenance basis — a base-side reference showing the defect predates the change, not just the assembler's say-so — plus a filed work-item id, carried to re-reviews through the settled-items channel, marked out of scope but accounted for. A pre-existing *blocking* finding is not transferable. Blame truth itself is judgment; the recorded attribution and its provenance basis are what stay mechanical and auditable.

**The bent ruler halts.** A finding that indicts the acceptance criteria or requirements themselves — as opposed to pre-existing artifact content — produces a halted verdict with an upstream-defect halt reason, never another round: every further round would measure against a ruler known to be bent. The campaign resumes only after the indicted upstream artifact has actually changed; a resume request over an unchanged ruler is refused. This is the concrete mechanism for "re-review in aggregate when original assumptions were incorrect."

**Fixer decisions are accepted within rounds.** No lens re-litigates a settled item; rebutted design-decision attacks enter the settled ledger like any rebuttal, and a finding matching a settled-ledger item does not re-enter the campaign at round assembly. Blocking-severity rebuttals accumulate into the terminal verdict for the human ruling.

**Termination has no numeric round cap.** A count cap reads the wrong signal — the observed campaigns fell 20→7→5 in count while severity rose. Termination is: terminal-clean (§5), or a **trend-analyst verdict**. A checkpoint fires after every second consecutive non-clean round since the last checkpoint, the first after round 2; a zero-blocking round exits to the terminal sweep instead, so no checkpoint ever follows a clean round. At each checkpoint a Fable-high trend-analysis dispatch reads the campaign — finding trends per lens, fix history, severity direction — and answers whether the findings indicate an upstream problem (missing/ambiguous ACs, design flaw) or a lens that is bikeshedding. Its recorded verdict is one of: **(a) continue two more rounds**, carrying staffing advice (a bikeshedding lens is de-staffed); **(b) terminate and bounce upstream** for remediation; **(c) terminate and escalate to the human**. The verdict cites the campaign evidence supporting it — the per-round trends, severity direction, and fix history it read — and an uncited verdict is invalid, resolving as (c). Severity rising while count falls forbids verdict (a): the analyst returns (b) or (c), never continuation. The checkpoint record is retained as a first-class campaign record alongside the staffing decisions; consumption is observable, because the next staffing record cites the checkpoint verdict and reflects its advice. The record also carries its origin — a returned verdict, or a dispatch failure — so a failure-escalation is distinguishable from an analyst-chosen one. Failure of the analyst dispatch or of its required inputs — retained verdicts missing, trend data malformed, the model unavailable — resolves as (c): the machine fails toward the human, never toward silent continuation. The analyst is the covert-case complement to the bent-ruler halt, which fires in-round when a single finding overtly indicts the upstream artifact. The circuit re-arm cap (§5) is a third firing condition: a cap-fired checkpoint admits only (b) or (c), and every terminate verdict — (b) or (c), from any checkpoint — carries the churn diagnosis of §5, invalid without it and resolving as (c). This is a standing Fable-high spend, bounded to campaigns that have already burned two rounds without cleaning; it is the deliberate top-tier-for-judgment seat, and this record — adopted by the owner — is the standing authorization the deployed delegation rule requires before a Fable spawn: the skill's dispatch cites it, and if that authorization is ever withdrawn the checkpoint resolves as (c).

## 8. Acceptance criteria

- **PANEL-B15** (§4): Every lens in a class roster declares `frontier` or `mid`; a roster containing an undeclared-tier lens is refused before any staffing, force-ceiling, or sweep determination reads it.

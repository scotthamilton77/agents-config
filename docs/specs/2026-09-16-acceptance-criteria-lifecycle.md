# Acceptance-criteria lifecycle: one standard, one home, attacked before a claim on work that ships a PR

**Date:** 2026-09-16
**Status:** Draft. Design child `agents-config-9k9.405.1` of the spec container `agents-config-9k9.405`, under the harness-rework milestone. Refines charter D1 and D3 and states where D3's attack fires for work that never has a spec.
**Relates to:** `agents-config-9k9.226` (the spec-quality standard, which cites the criteria standard this spec places), `agents-config-9k9.397` (the behavioural-outcome lens, slice S2 here), `agents-config-9k9.404` (slicing caught at creation and claim, the same doctrine at a different property), `agents-config-9k9.206` (an upstream lens at criteria authoring).

## Problem statement

Acceptance criteria are the termination condition a review checks against. They are the only thing that makes an LLM reviewer stop, because taste is inexhaustible and a contract is not. Today they enter the system through six doors and no door has a bouncer:

1. The grilling interview and its docs-attached variant refuse to end until criteria are enumerated.
2. The spec synthesis skill writes a spec's Acceptance Criteria section.
3. The ticketing skill and `work create --acceptance` copy criteria onto tracker items with no spec behind them.
4. `work deliver --spec` expands a spec's Continuations manifest into children, one criteria citation each.
5. `work acceptance set --why` restates criteria after work has started and records a trail.
6. A review-panel round halted on a criteria-indicting finding sends someone back to rewrite them.

Specs are not always written. The tracker holds 239 items carrying acceptance text. One attack record exists on the default branch. `work claim` refuses containers, blocked, parked, deferred and closed items and nothing else, so empty criteria and unattacked criteria both walk through to implementation.

Quality has no single source. Charter D1, the grilling skill, the spec skill and the attack skill's proposal sketch each carry a private copy of "red-test-convertible plus the edge-case taxonomy". None of them requires a criterion to name a behaviour a reader observes. PR 748's four criteria (a contract sentence exists, a test names it, the prompt bytes agree, the gate is green) satisfied every copy and three review rounds, and the behaviour change shipped unverified. The subagent-briefing skill tells a brief-writer that criteria are "tests that pass", which steers agents toward that artifact shape. The review-time rewrite has no guidance at all.

Storage has three copies during a review: the spec's criteria section, the tracker item's acceptance field, and the criteria file the review-panel invoker types by hand for `--acs`. The verdict-rendering work under `agents-config-9k9.390` caught the tracker copy one wording tweak behind the panel's file.

Two structural facts block the fix. The attack record binds by filename to the attacked document and is committed beside it; a tracker item has no beside. And the attack skill's doctrine says the attack runs before the work, never alongside it, so a criterion amended mid-execution is unattackable by the skill's own contract.

## Solution

One criteria standard, stated once and cited from every deployed skill that writes criteria. One canonical home, the tracker item's acceptance field, from which every consumer renders rather than retypes. One survival record per criterion set, stored where the record's existing binding rule already works. One gate, at claim, that refuses every leaf whose criteria are empty, and refuses a `feat` or `bugfix` leaf whose criteria are unattacked. One rule for amendment: an amended set is not used until it has been re-attacked, and the record's own revision binding is what enforces it.

## User stories

- **The implementer** claims a `feat` or `bugfix` leaf and knows the criteria on it were attacked, because the claim would have been refused otherwise. They render the criteria with one facade call and never retype them.
- **The spec author** writes criteria once, under one standard, and the children minted from the spec carry citations that render back to that text.
- **The ticket author** with no spec writes criteria on the item under the same standard, attacks them, and the item becomes claimable.
- **The reviewer after a halt** rewrites the criteria through `work acceptance set --why`, sees the survival record invalidate, and runs the attack again before the next round 1.
- **The brief writer** dispatching a subagent states criteria as observable outcomes and lists tests and gates as the evidence for them, not as the criteria.
- **The human** reading a posted verdict sees the criteria the round judged against, and they are the item's criteria, byte for byte.

## Decisions

**ACL-D1 — One criteria standard, one deployed home for it.** A shared skill, `acceptance-criteria`, is the single deployed statement of what a criterion is, and every deployed skill that writes or amends criteria cites it instead of carrying a copy. The standard: a criterion is an observable outcome. It names who observes (a user, a seat, a downstream reader, a gate) and what they see differently once the work is done. It is false today and true when the work is done, so it converts to one failing test or, where only a human at the surface can see the outcome, to one attributed observation; those are the two discharge states the evidence ledger already admits. A criterion set holds at least one behavioural criterion; a criterion that pins an artifact property (a sentence present, a constant set, a test naming a symbol, a byte pin) is admitted only beside a behavioural criterion it serves and says which. The edge-case taxonomy (inverse, empty and boundary, dependency failure, repeated and concurrent invocation, idempotency) is walked per criterion and each row resolved or ruled out with a reason. Of the six doors, four are deployed skills that write criteria (the grilling interview, its docs-attached variant, the spec skill, the ticketing skill); the briefing skill, the attack skill and the review-panel skill amend or judge them. Those seven cite the standard. The facade verbs among the doors are the home, not an authoring surface: their help states no definition and cites nothing. The taxonomy is stated in the standard; a lens mandate that names the taxonomy as the thing it walks is a citation, not a copy. The skill is shared, not Claude-only, because the four writing skills and the briefing skill deploy to every tool; the attack and review-panel skills are Claude-only and cite it there. It enters `src/` through the admission gate, and its admission argument is the deployed copies it deletes: the definition and taxonomy in the grilling and spec skills, and the taxonomy enumerated in the attack skill's own table. The charter's D1 is a dated record and stays. The spec-quality standard under `agents-config-9k9.226` cites this skill for the criteria clause rather than restating it.

**ACL-D2 — The tracker item's acceptance field is the canonical home.** The facade already names it as what a claim is checked against. It holds the criteria in full, citations by ID into the one spec the item names through `--spec`, or both in one field. A spec's criteria section is the authoring source for spec-born items and the manifest already copies citations onto children; nothing else copies text. Every consumer renders from the field through one facade verb, `work acceptance render ID`, which prints the item's description and criteria as Markdown, in field order, resolving each cited ID to the spec's full entry. The rendering is deterministic over description and criteria alone: the title is a label and is excluded, and a note on the item does not change the rendering, so repeated renders of unchanged data are byte-equal. Resolution rules: blank and whitespace-only lines are ignored; a line whose whole content matches the criterion-ID pattern is a citation, and any other line is text, whether or not the item names a spec; a citation resolves only against the spec the item names, never a global lookup, and resolution is single-pass, so resolved text is never read as a further citation; an ID that spec does not define, or defines more than once, or that the field cites more than once, is refused by that ID's name; a named spec that is missing, unreadable or unparseable is refused by its path and cause; a cited entry with no text renders as no criterion and the rest render in field order; a cited entry's text is preserved whole, continuation lines included. Criteria render as the `- **ID** text` bullets that spec-lint and the verdict poster already parse, so no second grammar exists between the render verb and its readers. The review-panel `--acs` file and the verdict poster's `--criteria` file are that verb's output, never a hand-typed file, and both refuse a file that is not byte-equal to a fresh rendering whenever the claim names a tracker item; a target with no item keeps a supplied file, as today.

**ACL-D3 — Each item has one attackable document, and the record lives beside it.** An item whose every non-blank criterion line is a citation into the spec it names is spec-born: its attackable document is that spec, its record is the spec's record beside the spec, and the spec's attack covers it. Changing the classification, or the named spec, changes the attackable document and so invalidates any attestation. Any other item, whether its criteria are full text or a mix of text and citations, is tracker-born: its attackable document is its own rendering from ACL-D2, written to `.work/attacks/<item-id>.md` and attacked as any document is, with the record beside it as `.work/attacks/<item-id>-ac-attack.json`. The attack checker's bind-by-name rule and its revision hashing work unchanged for both. A tracker-born item's two files are committed on the implementation branch before the claim. The `.work/attacks/` placement was decided over a note on the item, which is append-only bloat and the shape of the 64KB event-snapshot wall `agents-config-9k9.390` hit, and over a facade field, which is cleanest but a schema change nothing else needs. The directory is tracked, like the facade's own config beside it.

**ACL-D4 — A claim is refused on empty or unattacked criteria, and the attestation binds to the attackable document's content, not to note order.** The gate runs on the transition from `open` to `in_progress`; a claim on an item already in progress stays the existing no-op. `work claim` refuses a leaf whose criteria render to nothing, naming `work acceptance set`: an empty or whitespace-only field, or one whose citations resolve to no text. It refuses a leaf whose rendering is refused, naming the unresolved or duplicated ID, or the missing spec's path. It refuses a `feat` or `bugfix` leaf whose attackable document carries no attestation, naming the attack step. The attestation is one marker note, `[work] acceptance attacked <ISO-8601> <document-digest>`, written by a new verb `work acceptance attacked ID --record PATH`. That verb reads the record's proposals, dispositions and revisions, and refuses to write a marker when: the path is missing or does not parse as an attack record; the record's document is not the item's attackable document (the named spec for a spec-born item, the item's own rendering file for a tracker-born one); for a tracker-born item, that rendering file is not byte-equal to a fresh rendering; the round is open, meaning a proposal carries no disposition or the acceptances name differing revisions; or the closing revision, which is the acceptances' shared revision or the attacked revision when nothing was accepted, is not the digest of the attackable document as it stands. It does not re-derive the checker's other judgments, and the attack skill invokes it only after the checker exits zero. A second attestation with the same record is a no-op. At claim, the facade hashes the item's attackable document as it stands and looks for a marker carrying that digest; a marker whose timestamp or digest does not parse is not an attestation. Note order plays no part, so a restatement, a description edit on a tracker-born item, or an edit to the cited spec landing after the round and before the marker all leave the item unattested. Nouns: `feat` and `bugfix` need the attestation, because their delivery is a PR; `spike`, `chore` and `decision` need criteria and no attestation, because their delivery is a note or a decision; an item created `--trivial` is exempt from the attestation and not from the criteria, since the flag already carries the declaration that the work needs no design input, and a second flag for the same trust would drift from the first.

**ACL-D5 — Amended criteria are not used until re-attacked, and the record enforces it.** `work acceptance set --why` on a started tracker-born item changes its attackable document, so the committed record's closing revision no longer matches and the checker reports the round open. The CI gate (ACL-D6) goes red, and the item's attackable document carries no attestation, so the item cannot be claimed again once released. The same holds for a description edit on a tracker-born item, since the description is part of what the attackers read; that this forces a re-attack is intended and pinned, not an accident of the hashing. For a spec-born item the description is not attacked and its edit changes nothing; an edit to the cited spec is what invalidates: the spec's record stops closing and every citing item's attackable document changes digest. The agent runs a fresh attack round over the amended document, commits the new record over the old (git keeps the prior round), and attests again. The attack skill's doctrine sentence changes from "before the work, never alongside it" to "before the criteria are used, and again after any amendment"; its `--implementation-started` check is unchanged, since it already asks only that no round be open while work proceeds. A full round per amendment is the deliberate ceiling here; a delta-scoped attack is the upgrade if amendments turn out to be frequent.

**ACL-D6 — The attack checker is a CI gate over every record in the repository.** A `make` target runs the checker with `--implementation-started` over every attack record the tree holds, the ones beside specs and the ones under `.work/attacks/` alike, and joins the whole-repo gate. A record that does not close, or whose document has moved from the revision it attacked, is red and named. A record that does not parse is red and named, never skipped. A tree holding no records is green. What the gate cannot see is stated rather than hidden: a marker naming a digest for which no record was ever committed, or whose files were later deleted, passes the claim and this gate both. The attestation is exactly as strong as the PR number `work deliver --pr` records and the evidence ledger's `observed:` row: not unforgeable, but callable in, and visible in the branch's diff. Nothing retroactive: the 48 open items carrying criteria today are attacked when next claimed, not by a backfill.

**ACL-D7 — The review-time doors cite the standard and the home.** The review-panel skill says the criteria file is `work acceptance render` output, its emitter refuses a criteria file that is not byte-equal to a fresh rendering at round emission, and a criteria-indicting halt resolves through `work acceptance set --why` and a re-attack. The verdict poster refuses a criteria file that differs from a fresh rendering at posting time, so a criteria amendment between judgment and post refuses the post rather than posting text the round never judged. The subagent-briefing skill's criteria clause says criteria are observable outcomes per the standard, and tests, gates and artifacts are the evidence a report cites per criterion. `work discover` gains nothing: discovered items get criteria through `acceptance set` before claim, because the gate is at claim, not at creation.

**ACL-D8 — A stale facade is refused by name.** The attack skill's tracker-item path, the review-panel's render step and the verdict poster each check, before invoking a verb this spec introduces, that the facade on PATH reports a version at or past the one shipping it, and refuse naming that version otherwise. This is the same failure class the stale verdict poster had, and the same fix. A slice that lands a facade verb closes only when the facade on PATH reports that version. The claim gate is tool-agnostic and the attack skill is Claude-only: a session on another tool hands the attack round to a Claude Code session, exactly as the delivery contract already hands it the review round.

## Implementation decisions

- The rendering verb, the attestation verb and the claim refusals are facade changes in the `scotthamilton77/workcli` repository. Each slice here that needs one names the facade version that ships it and closes when the installed `work` reports that version.
- The attack skill gains no new storage code. The tracker-item path is documentation: render, attack the rendered file, commit both, attest.
- The behavioural-outcome lens is `agents-config-9k9.397` unchanged in scope; it becomes S2 of this spec and cites the standard rather than restating it.
- The CI target lives beside `spec-lint` and `doc-lint` in the root `Makefile`, invoking the deployed checker by its source path under `src/`.

## Testing decisions

Facade behaviour is tested in the workcli repository against its fake backend. The CI gate is tested by a fixture record under a temp directory. The standard's adoption is tested by the trigger evals the skill ships and by the attack lens of S2. Behavioural criteria below are discharged by tests where a test can observe the outcome and by an attributed observation where only a human at the surface can.

## Acceptance criteria

- **ACL-A1** An agent claiming an open `feat` or `bugfix` leaf whose attackable document carries no attestation is refused, and the refusal names the attack step; the same claim succeeds after the attack closes and attests; a `--trivial` leaf, and a `spike`, `chore` or `decision` leaf with criteria, claim without attestation; a spec-born child attests against the spec's own record. An attestation written before a restatement, a description edit on a tracker-born item, an edit to the cited spec, a change of the named spec, or a change of classification between spec-born and tracker-born no longer covers the item, whatever the note order; a description edit on a spec-born item leaves its attestation standing. A claim whose markers cannot be read is refused naming that failure, never granted. A claim on an item already in progress stays the existing no-op.
- **ACL-A2** An agent claiming any leaf whose criteria render to nothing is refused naming `work acceptance set`, whether the field is empty, whitespace-only, or a citation to an entry with no text; blank lines between criteria change neither the rendering nor the classification; a claim whose rendering is refused names the unresolved or duplicated ID, or the missing spec's path.
- **ACL-A3** An implementer who amends criteria on a started item sees the whole-repo gate go red on the unchanged record, naming it, and green again after a fresh round closes over the amended document; no other file changes between the two runs. The gate discovers every record in the tree, beside specs and under `.work/attacks/` alike, and names every invalid record in one run: a spec-born record whose spec moved, a committed record whose round never closed over an unchanged document, a record that does not parse; it is red naming the checker when the checker is absent from the tree, and red naming the location when a record location cannot be enumerated; a tree whose locations enumerate to no records is green.
- **ACL-A4** A reader of a posted verdict sees the criteria the round judged against byte-equal to the item's rendered criteria; a criteria file that differs from a fresh rendering is refused at round emission by the panel and at posting time by the poster, so an amendment between judgment and post refuses the post; an empty rendering is refused by both even when the file matches it; a panel or poster that cannot obtain the rendering for a claim naming a tracker item refuses naming the cause, never using the file unverified; a target with no tracker item keeps its supplied file in both.
- **ACL-A5** A tracker item citing spec IDs renders to the spec's full criterion text, continuation lines preserved, as `- **ID** text` bullets that spec-lint's and the verdict poster's parsers accept unchanged; a field mixing text and citations renders both in order, an empty cited entry omitted and the rest kept; a citation to an ID the named spec does not define, or defines twice, or that the field cites twice, is refused by that ID's name, even when another spec defines it; a named spec that is missing, unreadable or unparseable is refused by its path and cause, never read as a spec with no criteria; a citation-shaped line on an item with no spec, a text line on an item with one, and a line that merely embeds an ID, render as text; resolved text is never resolved again.
- **ACL-A6** A criterion set handed to the attack comes back with a finding naming each of: a set with no behavioural criterion; an artifact criterion whose served-criterion reference does not resolve to exactly one behavioural criterion in the set, whether missing, duplicated, itself, or an artifact criterion; a behavioural criterion that names no observer or no outcome that observer can distinguish; a criterion not observably false before the work; a criterion whose outcome converts to neither one failing test nor one attributed observation. A set whose behavioural criterion is well-formed on every count above and whose artifact criteria each resolve to it draws none of these. The record cannot close until each such finding is dispositioned. Registering the lens leaves no committed record open: each record in the tree closes again under a round of the new lens before the slice that registers it merges.
- **ACL-A7** An agent at any deployed skill that writes, amends, briefs, attacks or judges criteria, on any tool that deploys that skill, asked how to write a criterion reaches one statement of the standard; a listing of every tool's deployed configuration finds the criteria definition and the edge-case taxonomy each stated in exactly one asset, a citation of it in each of those seven skills at the point where it handles criteria, and none of the retired private phrasings in any of them, where a lens mandate naming the taxonomy as what it walks counts as a citation. The facade verbs among the doors carry no citation and no definition. This artifact criterion serves ACL-A6 and ACL-A8.
- **ACL-A8** A subagent brief produced under the briefing skill for work with several criteria and several tests states each criterion as an outcome a reader observes, carrying every rendered criterion assigned to that dispatch verbatim and nothing else as criteria, lists every test and gate under evidence mapped to the criterion it supports with no assigned criterion left without evidence, and introduces no test-passing or artifact-presence criterion; discharged by an `observed:` row in the evidence ledger naming a real dispatch after the skill change.
- **ACL-A9** A rendering of a tracker-born item carries the item's description verbatim before its criteria, and nothing in its place when the description is absent; it is unchanged by a note appended to that item or by a title change, so a record attacked before either still closes after it; two renders of unchanged data are byte-equal.
- **ACL-A10** An agent invoking the tracker-item attack path, the panel's render step or the verdict poster against a facade older than the one shipping the render and attest verbs and the claim refusals is refused naming the required version, not by an unknown-command error; the comparison follows semantic-version precedence, so the exact shipping version and later ones proceed while the version just below it and its prereleases refuse; a version command that fails, prints nothing, or prints something unparseable refuses naming that the version could not be established; the version read is the binary on PATH, never a checkout; and a slice landing any of those verbs closes only when `work --version` on PATH reports at or past the shipping version.
- **ACL-A11** An agent attesting an item is refused with no marker written when the record is missing or unparseable, when its document is not the item's attackable document, when a tracker-born item's rendering file is not byte-equal to a fresh rendering, when a proposal in it carries no disposition or its acceptances name differing revisions, or when its closing revision is not the digest of the attackable document as it stands; a record whose proposals were all rejected closes at its attacked revision; an item is spec-born only when every non-blank criterion line is a citation into its named spec, and one full-text line makes it tracker-born; the same record attests once those hold, attesting twice with it changes nothing, and two concurrent attestations leave one marker.
- **ACL-A12** A spec author who edits a criterion in a merged spec sees every child `work deliver --spec` minted from it and citing that ID render the new text with no edit to the child, because the child carries a citation and not a copy, while children citing other IDs render byte-equal to before; every spec-born child of that spec attests against the spec's own record, and the edit leaves all of them unattested until the spec is re-attacked.
- **ACL-A13** An agent following the deployed attack skill's tracker-item path for a tracker-born item ends with the current rendering at `.work/attacks/<item-id>.md`, the closed record beside it, and one attestation carrying that rendering's digest; the skill states that path and the doctrine that the attack runs before the criteria are used and again after any amendment, with no sentence saying it never runs alongside the work; an agent reading the deployed review-panel skill finds that the criteria file is render output, never hand-typed, and that a criteria-indicting halt resolves through `work acceptance set --why` and a re-attack; an agent reading this repository's delivery contract finds the `.work/attacks/` convention, and its glossary's acceptance-criterion entry points at the standard. This artifact criterion serves ACL-A3 and ACL-A4.
- **ACL-A14** The `acceptance-criteria` skill carries a complete admission record naming the deployed copies it replaces, its cost and its removal condition, and the installer's staging check lists it in every supported tool's configuration directory. This artifact criterion serves ACL-A7.

## Out of scope

- A delta-scoped re-attack after amendment; the full round is the ceiling, stated at ACL-D5.
- A facade field holding the attack record or its digest beyond the marker note.
- The facade re-deriving the checker's judgments beyond the four refusals ACL-D4 names, or verifying that a record's files are committed; the CI gate owns the rest and the branch diff shows the files.
- The facade's own help text for criteria-bearing verbs; it states no definition today and the standard is cited from the skills that write criteria.
- Transactions spanning two backend writes: a claim, an attestation, a round emission or a post each hashes or compares at its own moment, and an amendment landing after that moment is caught by the next gate (ACL-D5, ACL-D6), not by serializing the two.
- The spec-quality standard's citation of this one, which `agents-config-9k9.226` owns.
- A CI inventory of criteria consumers; a new consumer enters through the admission gate.

## Further notes

The gate sits at claim rather than at creation because creation has six doors and claim has one. A door can stay wide, and an item can hold rough criteria while it waits, so long as nothing executes against them until they survive an attack. The attestation is asked only of the nouns whose delivery is a PR, and `--trivial` is the one exemption within those: it is the flag that already says this work will not be designed, and the claim gate reads the flag rather than minting a second one.
